## The question

California's investor-owned utilities recover most of their wildfire mitigation spending through customer rates. As wildfires grow more frequent and severe, those costs keep rising, and they fall hardest on households that already struggle to pay their bills. OEIS asked how the state could fund a more resilient grid without making electricity less affordable.

## What I did

1. **Ratemaking reforms.** I assessed the reforms already under way to how wildfire costs are built into rates, weighing their strengths, weaknesses, and effects on affordability.
2. **Alternative financing.** With my teammates, I compared other ways to share the cost, including taxes, municipal fees, public-private partnerships, insurance adjustments, and federal and state grants, and looked at what California could learn from other states.
3. **Executive summary.** I wrote the summary that distilled our recommendations for the client.

- **Every alternative moves the bill somewhere.** Shifting costs off rates helps low-income customers but competes with other public spending; keeping them on rates works only if the financing gets cheaper.
