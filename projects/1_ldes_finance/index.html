Financing Long-Duration Storage in California

A 20-year project finance model testing which policies get a 500 MW / 50 GWh iron-air battery to financial close.

Individual project · “Financing the Clean Energy Transition” class project, Harvard Kennedy School, 2026

The question

No long-duration storage project has reached financial close in California, even though the state will need multi-day storage to run a carbon-free grid. Which combination of policies would make one financeable, and what would it cost ratepayers?

What I did

I built a 20-year project finance model for a 500 MW / 50 GWh iron-air battery, with costs benchmarked to Form Energy’s project in Minnesota. It covers capital and operating costs, energy arbitrage and capacity revenue, three tranches of term debt, depreciation, and taxes, and solves for the sponsor equity each scenario needs. I tested five policies: the federal investment tax credit, a CPUC-procured clean firm capacity contract, a DOE loan guarantee, an O&M subsidy tied to delivered capacity, and a $100M capital grant.

Equity IRR across capacity contract prices under both ITC levels, with the other four policies in place.

What it shows

  • With all five policies and a $240/kW-yr capacity contract, the project clears its lenders’ test: minimum debt service coverage of 1.31× against a 1.20× requirement.
  • Equity returns hinge on the capacity contract. Reaching a 17.5% equity return takes about $310/kW-yr with a 50% ITC, and about $387/kW-yr with the 40% credit.
  • Because ratepayers would pay that contract, its size is the central question for the CPUC.
